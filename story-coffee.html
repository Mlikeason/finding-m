The red coffee machine

红色咖啡机

中文

这台红色的拉玛佐咖啡机，是买给我老婆的生日礼物。

这可能是这辈子买给她最重的一件礼物了。感觉差不多有半个我那么重。每次要把它抱起来放到桌上，都会觉得肩膀快断了。送礼物的时候，通常想的是对方喜不喜欢，很少会想到，自己到底搬不搬得动。

我老婆是一个相对爱喝咖啡的人。我们在各种咖啡店里徘徊的时候，她也会看人家的咖啡机，觉得这个很好，那个也很好。有时候，我们会去高岛屋地下的商场，看看不同的机器。咖啡还没喝多少，机器倒是看了不少。

所以在那次生日到来之前，我已经早早把咖啡机写进了礼物清单。找了 La Marzocco 的线下店，准备去当面看看。几个型号，哪种大小合适，再选个颜色。其实它的各种颜色都挺好看的，这也是一个问题。

结果发现，那家店离我们家也就两个街口。去了以后，反而很爽快，大概十分钟就定下来了，之后送到了家里。

有些东西，买之前可以想很久，真到了眼前，决定却不需要那么长时间。这一次就是这样。最后选了红色，送来之后，家里一下子添色不少。

其实我们俩平时不太会在家里突然放这么浓重的颜色。两个人都是设计师，对家里的色彩布局、小物件之间怎么搭配，多少有些讲究。不是说每一样都要多贵，而是放在一起，自己看着得舒服。这么大面积的红色，我们还是头一次用。

但红色搭配它那么硬朗的线条，确实很好看。咖啡机又是早上用的东西，想一想，每天开始的时候能看见这么一块颜色，感觉也不错。它不是那种你用完了，会顺手收进柜子里的小家电。放在那里，就一直在那里。

后来，它在我们家客厅占据了一个很重要的角落。任何来家里的人，基本都不会忽略它的存在。红得那么认真，想忽略也挺难的。

它的价格确实挺贵。

我们之前用的是一台 SMEG 咖啡机，和这一台完全不是一个价位。这台 La Marzocco 算下来，要五万多人民币。真要按一天做多少杯咖啡来计算，恐怕很难算出一个特别划算的结果。

我们家每天的咖啡需求并不大。早上可能一杯、两杯，有客人来，再多做两杯。仅此而已，而且也不是每天都会做。你要说这台机器在我们家大材小用，那确实是。买回来之前也知道，不是用了以后才发现的。

不过，家里的东西，有时候又不能只按使用次数来算。要是每一样都得把它的能力用尽，才能证明自己买得对，那过日子也挺累的。至少这台咖啡机，我没有打算让它每天替我证明什么。

每天看见它，会觉得生活还是很美好。也会有一点很俗气、但很真实的满足感：我们好像还是有那么一点家底，能够用得起比较上档次、比较讲究的东西。

这么说可能没有什么特别高明的审美，也不需要。我就是会因为家里有一件自己喜欢、也知道它确实很好的东西，而觉得高兴。这种满足感，有时候比喝到那一口咖啡还多。

当然，它毕竟是送给老婆的生日礼物。放在家里，她做咖啡，我也跟着喝，然后两个人都从里面得到一点快乐。这个礼物倒也没有白买。

我其实是个不太喝咖啡的人。但是老婆在家做的拿铁，还是不错的。她比较知道我喜欢甜到什么程度，不多不少，刚好合我的口味。

有时候，自己喜欢一杯咖啡，原因并没有那么复杂。不一定是能喝出多少层次，也不一定知道豆子有什么了不起。就是端过来，喝一口，觉得正好。有人知道你说的“正好”是什么程度，这件事本身也挺好。

自从有了这台咖啡机，我零零散散也开始主动买一些咖啡豆回家。路过不同的店，看到有豆子卖，包装还不错，感觉自己喜欢，就会买一包回来给她试试。我的挑选方法谈不上多专业，有时候确实先看包装。两个人都是设计师的家庭，在这种地方也很难完全不受影响。

有的豆子试过一次，就再也不买了；有的则会反复买。试得多了，才慢慢知道自己喜欢什么。以前在咖啡店里，这杯喝完就喝完了，现在偶尔会多留意一点：这家有没有卖豆子，要不要带一点回去。

新加坡有一个牌子叫 PPP，三个P。他们家的咖啡豆我很喜欢，只要路过那家店，基本都会买一小包回来。

买多了也不行，新加坡太容易受潮。所以每次就买小包，喝完再买。家里咖啡喝得本来就不快，没必要因为喜欢，就一下子囤很多。机器已经买得够大了，豆子还是可以少买一点。

去其他国家出差的时候，如果想不到有什么伴手礼可带，我也会买一两包咖啡豆回来。不是每一包都会喜欢，但总可以试一试。相比买一件不知道放在哪里的小东西，咖啡豆有一个很明确的去处：带回家，放到那台红色机器旁边。

我有时候会觉得，至少对我来说，咖啡真不是一种从小就有的习惯。不是天生知道它好喝在哪里，也不是一开始就会想要每天来一杯，都是后来一点点试、一点点培养出来的。

也不一定是为了附庸风雅。就是试着试着，发现早上除了牛奶、除了那杯冰水，还可以有一样自己爱喝的东西。至于这个习惯是从哪一天开始的，反而没有那么重要。

早上有一杯合自己口味的冰咖啡，作为一天的开始，感觉还是挺棒的。接下来又有新的事情要做，新的问题要处理，也许还有一些不一样的机会。一杯咖啡当然不能帮你把这些事情都解决，但至少出门之前，会觉得今天还不错。

家里那台红色的机器，就在它自己的角落里。用得不算多，搬起来又重，价钱也谈不上便宜。但想到它，想到老婆做的那杯拿铁，还是会觉得，生活很值得一过。

English

This red La Marzocco was a birthday present for my wife.

It may be the heaviest present I have ever bought her. It feels as though it weighs about half as much as I do. Every time I have to lift it onto the counter, I feel my shoulders might give out. When choosing a present, you usually wonder whether the other person will like it. You rarely stop to ask whether you can actually carry it.

My wife is quite fond of coffee. Whenever we wander into cafés, she also looks at their machines: this one is nice, that one is nice too. Sometimes we go to the basement of Takashimaya to look at different machines. We have probably looked at more machines than we have drunk cups of coffee.

So well before that birthday, a coffee machine was already on my gift list. I found a La Marzocco showroom and planned to have a look in person: which model, which size, and then which colour. All the colours looked rather good, which was a problem in itself.

It turned out that the shop was only about two blocks from home. Once there, I made the decision surprisingly quickly—within about ten minutes. The machine was delivered afterwards.

Some things take a long time to think about before buying, but very little time to decide on once they are in front of you. This was one of those purchases. I chose red, and when it arrived, the house suddenly had a lot more colour.

Neither of us would normally introduce such a strong colour into our home. We are both designers, so we care about how colours and small objects sit together. That does not mean everything has to be expensive. We just want to feel comfortable looking at it all. This was our first time using quite so much red.

But the red works beautifully with those strong, angular lines. And a coffee machine is something you use in the morning. The thought of seeing that colour at the beginning of a day felt rather nice. It is not a small appliance you put back in a cupboard when you are done. Once it has its place, it stays there.

It came to occupy an important corner of our living room. Almost anyone who visits notices it. It is so emphatically red that ignoring it would take some effort.

It was certainly expensive.

We previously used a SMEG machine, which was in an entirely different price bracket. This La Marzocco worked out at more than fifty thousand renminbi. If I calculated its value by the number of coffees we make each day, I would struggle to call it a bargain.

Our daily demand for coffee is modest. One or two cups in the morning, perhaps a couple more when friends visit. That is about it, and we do not even make coffee every day. If you think the machine is overqualified for our household, you are right. We knew that before buying it; it was not something we discovered afterwards.

But things at home cannot always be judged by how often we use them. If every object had to be worked to the limit of its capabilities to prove that buying it was justified, life would become quite tiring. I have never expected this machine to justify itself to me every day.

Seeing it makes me feel that life is good. There is also a slightly materialistic, but very real, satisfaction in it: we seem to have a little something to our name, enough to afford things that are well made and a little more refined.

That may not sound like a particularly sophisticated aesthetic judgment. It does not need to be. Having something at home that I like, and know to be good, simply makes me happy. Sometimes that satisfaction is greater than the pleasure of the coffee itself.

Of course, it was a birthday present for my wife. She makes coffee, I get to drink some too, and in the end we both get a little happiness out of it. The present has certainly not gone to waste.

I am not actually much of a coffee drinker. But the lattes my wife makes at home are very good. She knows how sweet I like them: not too much, not too little, just right for me.

Sometimes liking a cup of coffee is not complicated. It does not have to mean identifying layers of flavour or knowing what makes the beans remarkable. Someone hands you a cup, you take a sip, and it is just right. Having someone who knows exactly what you mean by “just right” is a good thing in itself.

Since we bought the machine, I have gradually started bringing home coffee beans myself. If I pass a shop selling them, and the packaging looks nice and appeals to me, I will buy a bag for her to try. My selection process is hardly professional. Sometimes I really do start with the packaging. In a household of two designers, it is difficult to be entirely immune to that.

Some beans we try once and never buy again. Others we keep coming back to. After trying enough, you begin to understand what you like. A cup in a café used to end when I finished drinking it. Now I sometimes pay a little more attention: do they sell their beans, and should I take some home?

There is a brand in Singapore called PPP—three Ps. I really like their beans. Whenever I pass the shop, I almost always bring home a small bag.

Buying too much does not work either. Things get damp easily in Singapore, so I buy small bags and replenish them when they run out. We do not get through coffee very quickly in the first place. Liking something is no reason to stockpile it. The machine is already big enough; at least I can be restrained about the beans.

When I travel to other countries for work and cannot think of a gift to bring home, I sometimes buy a bag or two of coffee beans. We will not like every bag, but we can always try it. Unlike a little souvenir with no obvious place to go, the beans have a clear destination: home, beside the red machine.

At least for me, coffee was not a habit I grew up with. I did not instinctively know what was good about it, nor did I want a cup every day from the beginning. It was something I came to gradually, by trying it and getting used to it.

It does not have to be about acquiring a sophisticated taste. After a while, you simply find that alongside milk or a glass of iced water, there is another drink you enjoy in the morning. Exactly when that habit began does not really matter.

Starting the day with an iced coffee made the way you like it feels pretty good. There will be things to do, new problems to deal with, perhaps some different opportunities. A cup of coffee cannot sort all of that out for you, of course. But before you leave the house, you can at least feel that the day is off to a decent start.

The red machine is there, in its own corner. We do not use it all that much. It is heavy to move, and it was certainly not cheap. But thinking of it, and of the latte my wife makes, I still feel that life is well worth living.
