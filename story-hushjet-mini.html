HUSHJET MINI

HUSHJET MINI 随身风扇

中文

现在的工业设计产品，戴森毫无疑问是行业的先驱。除了贵，没有毛病。

十几年前我买的第一款戴森，就是那个放在桌上的圆形无叶风扇，现在还放在我妈妈家里，好好地用着。对于戴森的产品，不需要问，先买了再说。

但实际上有目共睹，戴森产品的质量也是很有问题的，比较容易坏。像吸尘器，保修期一过，一两年左右就肯定会出问题，需要维修或者换零件。

可它精湛的设计和那些让人出乎意料的创意，总让人忍不住想消费，想把家里的旧电器换成戴森。它同时也变成了一种现代生活方式的代表：一个装修很现代、很时髦的家里，如果没几件戴森的产品，格调好像总差那么一点。不知不觉，家里就有了各种各样的戴森。

我甚至在商场用公共厕所的时候，都很想把戴森那个巨大的干手机买回家。但噪音实在太大，放在家里的实际意义也没有那么大。不过工业设计确实很好看。

说回来，这次要说的是戴森的随身小风扇，HushJet Mini。HushJet 这个名字，就是戴森一贯的品牌形象：强吸力、强压缩，出风量大。不管电吹风还是吸尘器，基本都是这个逻辑。只要是出风或者吸风的东西，就是它的核心竞争力。

这样一台小风扇，市面上普通的十几块、二十块、三十块人民币就能买到。戴森出了一个，卖 150 新币，折合人民币小一千。国内卖 799 还是 699，我记不清了，反正差不多是同类产品的十倍价格。戴森的定价，基本上就是同类普通产品的十倍。

我觉得还挺好看的，虽然很多人觉得它的样子比较怪异。在热带旅行，很多人都会随身带这样一个小风扇，太热的时候就举着吹，或者给孩子吹。所以很多人会觉得，来新加坡、泰国、马来西亚、印尼旅行，这样的小风扇是必需品。

但其实在新加坡生活的人都知道，在街上拿着小风扇的，一看就是游客，不是本地人。当地人早就习惯了这样的气候，绝对不会用这种东西。我现在也差不多算是个本地人了。如果不是戴森出了这样一个小风扇，我对这个类别的产品根本没有兴趣。

风力很强，但风力强带来的问题就是噪音肯定很大，这一点避免不了。不过设计确实非常精致，出风头还可以旋转，拿在手上对着自己吹，也可以挂在脖子上，把风扇调成垂直向上，吹自己的脖子。很喜欢，爱不释手。放在桌面上充电的那个小底座也挺可爱。

但买了之后，我好像一次都没有带出去过，都放在家里。出门实在不想再多带一件东西。即使去别的海岛旅行，出门也总是忘了带它。到了真正要用的时候，才发现它还放在家里。我承认它很有实用性，但对我来说，实用性好像没有那么强。

不过它有一个很有用的功能：可以用来做礼物。一个小盒子，150 新币，不算很贵，又是一件戴森的产品，很体面，很好看。所以我考虑，以后见朋友的时候，可以多把它当作礼物送给大家。毕竟不是每个人都会买这样一件东西放在家里。送礼物最怕对方已经有了，但这件东西我相信，绝大部分人是不会买的，绝大部分理智的人是不会买的。

总之，一直生活在东南亚，365 天都是夏天，热不热呢？当然热。但大部分时间我们都在室内、在车里、在地下，不会有太长的时间一定要在路上走来走去。所以风扇这件事，还好。

倒是我之前在日本买过一个索尼的设备，贴在脖子后面用来降温的，挺有意思。它能直接给脊柱提供一个很冰的温度，很凉爽。那个也许比风扇更有用，也更隐蔽一些。

不过说到底，买戴森的东西，主要还是为它的工业设计买单。你看着喜欢，就愿意花钱。用不用，再说。

English

When it comes to industrial design, Dyson is unquestionably the pioneer of the industry. Apart from being expensive, there is nothing wrong with it.

The first Dyson I bought, more than ten years ago, was the round bladeless fan that sits on a desk. It is still at my mother's place, working perfectly. With Dyson, you don't ask questions. You buy first.

But the truth is plain to see: Dyson's build quality is also genuinely problematic. Things break easily. A vacuum cleaner, for instance, will almost certainly develop a fault a year or two after the warranty expires, and need a repair or a replacement part.

And yet its exquisite design and those unexpected ideas always make you want to spend, want to replace the old appliances at home with Dyson. It has also become a symbol of a modern way of life: in a very modern, very stylish home, if you don't have a few Dyson products, the taste somehow feels incomplete. Before I knew it, we had all kinds of Dyson products at home.

I have even stood in a shopping mall restroom, looking at Dyson's enormous hand dryer, wanting to buy one and bring it home. But the noise is far too loud, and at home it wouldn't really serve much purpose. The industrial design is beautiful, though.

Anyway, what I want to talk about is Dyson's little portable fan, the HushJet Mini. HushJet is Dyson's brand image in a name: powerful suction, strong compression, a big volume of air. Whether it is a hair dryer or a vacuum cleaner, the logic is basically the same. Anything that blows air out or sucks it in — that is its core strength.

A fan like this, an ordinary one, costs ten, twenty, thirty yuan on the market. Dyson made one and sells it for S$150, just under a thousand yuan. In China it sells for 799 or 699, I can't remember which. Either way, it is roughly ten times the price of an ordinary product. That is how Dyson prices things: about ten times the ordinary product in the category.

I think it looks quite good, although many people find its shape rather odd. Travelling in the tropics, many people carry a small fan like this, holding it up when it gets too hot, or pointing it at their child. So a lot of people think a small fan is a must-have when you travel to Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia.

But anyone who lives in Singapore knows: if you see someone on the street holding a small fan, that's a tourist, not a local. Locals are long used to the climate and would never use such a thing. I am more or less a local myself now. If Dyson hadn't made this little fan, I would have no interest in this kind of product at all.

The airflow is very strong, but strong airflow brings a problem that can't be avoided: the noise is definitely loud. The design, though, is genuinely exquisite. The fan head rotates. You can hold it in your hand and point it at yourself, or hang it around your neck and tilt it straight up to blow at your neck. I love it. I can't put it down. And the little base it sits on to charge on the desk is rather cute too.

But since I bought it, I don't think I have taken it out even once. It just sits at home. Going out, I really don't want to carry one more thing. Even when we travel to other islands, I always forget to bring it. By the time I actually want to use it, I realise it is still sitting at home. I admit it is useful, but for me, perhaps not that useful.

It does have one very good use, though: it makes a gift. A small box, S$150, not too expensive, and it is a Dyson product. Presentable, good-looking. So I am thinking I might give it as a gift more often, when I meet friends. After all, not everyone would buy such a thing for themselves. The thing you worry about most when giving a gift is that the other person already has one. But this, I believe, is something the vast majority of people would never buy. The vast majority of sensible people would never buy it.

In the end, living in Southeast Asia, it is summer 365 days a year. Is it hot? Of course it is hot. But most of the time we are indoors, in the car, underground. There isn't much time when you absolutely have to be walking around outside. So a fan, really, is fine to do without.

What I did buy in Japan, though, was a Sony device that you stick on the back of your neck to cool you down. That was quite interesting. It gives your spine a directly icy temperature, very refreshing. That might be more useful than a fan, and more discreet too.

But when all is said and done, buying Dyson is mainly paying for the industrial design. If you like the look of it, you are willing to pay. Whether you use it or not, that comes later.
