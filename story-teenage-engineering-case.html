T E BOX

T E BOX

中文

我已经很多很多年没有自己组装过台式电脑了，我甚至不确定自己还记不记得怎么安装主板、CPU、风扇、电源、内存、硬盘。十几年前，我甚至以为自己这辈子再也不会装台式机了。

没想到去年连续装了两台。第一台是为了玩《黑神话：悟空》这个著名的游戏，买了一张很好的显卡，装了一台所有零件外露的开放式机箱。然后在装这台机器的过程中，我突然看到了一个非常喜欢的 IT 品牌，叫做 Teenage Engineering。他们也出了一款机箱。于是我这个设计师的老毛病又犯了，纯粹为了拥有这款机箱，而去为它装了一台电脑，很有买椟还珠的感觉。

他们做的这台机箱，其实只是几块很平的金属件。寄过来的时候就是几块平板，需要自己去钣金、折叠安装。

我不得不说，他们的颜色调得非常漂亮。这个橙色，还有另外一款白色，我怎么看都很美。既有机器和金属那种硬质钢印的感觉，又有柔和的色调，两者结合得非常完美。售价也不贵，我想买的时候，他们美国官网正好在特价，99 美金。

拿到手之前，我就开始为它采购特定的小主板、小显卡。他们说这个机箱装完之后，装不下性能特别强劲的配件。还好，现在硬盘都是 SSD，不用再插一块大的 3.5 寸硬盘了。我当年装机的时候，硬盘、光驱，甚至软驱都要装进去，是一台特别笨重、特别丑的机器。到今天，机箱居然能变得这么漂亮，实在让我觉得这个世界真是非常发达。

机箱装好之后，我把它放在了办公室里，放在公司电脑、公司显示器旁边。我日常用 MacBook，偶尔需要用 Windows 的时候，就打开这台机器。性能不怎么样，但 CPU 配得还可以，内存比较大，显卡虽然差，整体用起来还是 OK 的。再装上我非常喜欢的 Windows 11。虽然如今大家都习惯了用 Mac，但实际上还是有很多事情只能 Windows 做。我之前在微软工作过一段时间，也做过 Windows 相关的设计，所以对 Windows 本身还是非常有感情的。

在微软的生态里，能把产品做得这么漂亮，卖得也不贵，我觉得已经非常非常不错了。它让我经常思考，生活中其实有很多我们认为很多年一成不变的产品，其实有机会设计得更加富有吸引力、富有感染力，只是看我们愿不愿意牺牲一些东西。

一台电脑机箱，如果你是为了最完整的体验、最强劲的性能去设计，它肯定不能设计得这么优美。想把它设计得小巧精致优美，就肯定要牺牲一些性能。虽然它没有做到 Mac mini 那么小的尺寸，但对于 Windows 来说，在桌上巴掌大的地方能实现这个，我觉得已经算是比较均衡了。基本上，只要你不玩游戏，这台机器的性能应该是足够的。我配的是 AMD 的处理器，内存应该是 64G，硬盘是 SSD，其实就差不多了，就这些东西。

如今台式机的主板都带有无线 WiFi，非常方便。当我真的需要把它提回家用的时候，它上面有两个不锈钢提手，也很方便。我甚至没有给这台机箱装风扇，既然没有大的显卡在里面，我觉得装额外的风扇已经无所谓了。

在我还在读大学的时候，装电脑是一门技能。很多同班同学、同寝室的同学，甚至很多女生，都会需要你帮忙装一台电脑，送到宿舍里。你甚至可能因此改变一生，认识那个女孩。

到现在，大家都是直接买一台笔记本。这世界变化很快，20 多年过去，却仍然有机会让我返璞归真，回到当年，动手装主板、拧螺丝、插线。这个过程里，有非常复杂的回忆和情感因素在里面。

我甚至在想，我在安装的过程中，并不是为了装完之后能用上它，而仅仅是把这个过程重新再做一次、再走一遍，就仿佛回到了当年那条路，再走一遍。人并没有很多机会能重新活一次。是这样一些奇妙的产品，帮我回到了当年那个时空。

再静下心来，认认真真地思考硬件的兼容性，该插哪一条线，正插还是反插，插完之后怎么从零开始安装系统、安装驱动。尽管现在基本上不需要单独安装驱动这个流程了。

还是很有年轻的感觉。我是一个设计师，但这么看来，同样也是一个年轻的工程师。所以难怪 Teenage Engineering 这个品牌非常讨我喜欢，只可惜它的其他很多产品，确确实实不太用得上。

English

I haven't built a desktop computer myself for many, many years. I wasn't even sure I still remembered how to install a motherboard, a CPU, a fan, a power supply, memory, a hard drive. More than ten years ago, I thought I would never build a desktop again.

Then last year I ended up building two in a row. The first was for Black Myth: Wukong, that famous game. I bought a very good graphics card and built an open-frame case, with all the parts exposed. While I was building it, I suddenly came across an IT brand I really like, called Teenage Engineering. They make a computer case too. And my old designer habit kicked in: purely to own this case, I built a whole computer for it. It felt very much like buying the box for the pearl inside.

The case they make is really just a few flat pieces of metal. When it arrives, it's a few flat sheets, and you have to bend and fold them into shape yourself.

I have to say, their colours are beautifully done. This orange, and the other one in white — however I look at them, they're beautiful. There's exactly that hard, stamped-metal feeling of a machine, combined with soft tones. A perfect combination. And it isn't expensive. Just when I decided I wanted to buy one, their US website had it on sale for $99.

Before it even arrived, I started buying the specific small motherboard and small graphics card it would need. They say that once it's built, you can't put particularly powerful parts inside it. Luckily, hard drives are all SSDs now, so there's no need for a big 3.5-inch drive. Back when I used to build computers, you had to fit a hard drive, an optical drive, even a floppy drive — a very heavy, very ugly machine. That a case can be this beautiful today really makes me feel how far the world has come.

Once it was built, I put it in my office, next to my work computer and work monitor. I use a MacBook day to day, and when I occasionally need Windows, I switch this machine on. The performance isn't great, but the CPU I chose is decent, there's plenty of memory, and although the graphics card is weak, overall it works fine. And I installed Windows 11, which I really like. These days everyone is used to the Mac, but in reality there are still many things only Windows can do. I worked at Microsoft for a while, and did Windows-related design, so I still have a lot of affection for Windows itself.

Within the Microsoft ecosystem, to make a product this beautiful and not expensive — I think that is already very, very good. It often makes me think: there are so many products in life that we assume haven't changed for years, but they could actually be designed to be more attractive, more affecting. It just depends on whether we're willing to give something up.

If you design a computer case for the most complete experience and the most powerful performance, it certainly can't look this beautiful. If you want it small, refined and beautiful, you have to sacrifice some performance. It isn't as small as a Mac mini, but for Windows, to achieve this in a space the size of your palm on a desk — I think that's fairly balanced. Basically, as long as you don't play games, this machine's performance should be enough. I chose an AMD processor, the memory is probably 64GB, and the drive is an SSD, as I said. That's really about it.

Desktop motherboards today all come with wireless Wi-Fi, which is very convenient. And when I actually need to carry it home to use, it has two stainless steel handles, also very convenient. I didn't even put a fan in this case — since there's no big graphics card inside, I felt there was no need for extra fans.

Back when I was at university, building a computer was a skill. Many classmates, many roommates, even many girls, would need you to help build a computer and carry it to their dorm. You might even change your whole life that way, and meet that girl.

Now everyone just buys a laptop. The world changes fast, but more than twenty years on, I still got the chance to return to the basics — to go back to those days, installing a motherboard, tightening screws, plugging in cables, with my own hands. That process carries very complicated memories and emotions.

I sometimes think that while I was building it, I wasn't doing it to use the machine afterwards, but simply to go through the process again, to walk it one more time — as if going back to that road from years ago and walking it again. People don't get many chances to live their life over. It is these wonderful products that helped me return to that time and place.

And to calm down and think carefully about hardware compatibility — which cable goes where, which way round it goes, and how to install the system and the drivers from scratch afterwards. Even though these days you basically don't need to install drivers separately any more.

It still feels young. I'm a designer, but looking at it this way, I'm also a young engineer. No wonder I like a brand like Teenage Engineering so much. It's just a shame that many of their other products are things I really can't use.
