Walkmans through the years

岁月的随身听

中文

我记得高中的时候，我存了三年的钱，最后买下一台索尼收录放一体的随身听，在当时算是很高级的机器了。那个年代的男生，好像多少都做过一个随身听的梦。后来世界变得很快，磁带之后是CD，CD之后是MD，然后MP3出来了，到现在大家都用手机，在线听流媒体，这些老物件好像只剩下我们年轻时候的一些零散故事了。

不得不说，索尼在随身听的设计和工艺上，确实做到了登峰造极，很多机器现在拿出来把玩一下，还是会被那种做工打动。小时候那些磁带大部分都发了霉，不太能听了，可我还是喜欢收一些随身听，都是当年很喜欢、却买不起或者买不到的型号。其实这些机器现在拿在手上已经没什么用处，磁带随身听里面有一根很关键的皮带，橡胶做的，时间一长就会老化、松掉，有的甚至化成黏糊糊的一摊，机器也就转不起来了。

早些年还在北京生活的时候，我零零散散收了很多台。小时候看起来特别珍贵的东西，现在因为互联网，基本上都有机会重新买到，当然，你愿意付多少钱，也就决定了你能买到什么样的成色。我在北京有一位朋友，索尼随身听的各个系列、各种颜色，他差不多收齐了，全是全新带盒的，磁带机、CD机、MD都有。他在北京没有买房，把买房的钱全都放进了收藏室，这也算是一种很高尚的理想了。

说到底，随身听只是拿来听音乐的。现在想听哪一首歌，点一下就有，什么都触手可得。当年为了听某一首歌，得按住倒带键，估摸着差不多了松开，放一两句，不对，再倒，那种一点一点去找的感觉，现在已经没有了。听磁带的时候还有一个常用的动作，拿一支铅笔插进磁带的孔里，捏着悬空甩，磁带就跟着转，能倒得快一点，那个时候觉得特别好玩。

磁带现在是不能听了，CD我倒是一直还在买，家里收了很多，有几张特别喜欢的，我挂在了墙上。在我看来，在线的流媒体终究是虚无缥缈的，歌说下架就下架，CD是一张实实在在的光盘，放在我自己家里，想听的时候随时拿出来，永远都在。当年买磁带的时候，我大概也是这么想的。

其实我的第一台随身听，不是自己攒钱买的。刚上高一那年，父亲省吃俭用，给我买了一台索尼最入门的单放机，很厚很大，1996年，两百多块钱，那时候城里一个普通职工，一个月也就挣四五百块。跟我后来收的那些机器比起来，它笨重、粗糙，可我到现在还能清楚地想起来，那天下午拿到它的时候有多激动，好像有什么事情要从那天开始了。

我人生里的很多故事，追根溯源都和音乐有关。以前听歌，后来玩乐队，认识了很多人，也因为这些人，才有了后来的职业生涯。说不定这一切的起头，就是那台两百多块钱的随身听呢。它就在配图里倒数第二行的最右边，夹在一堆做工精致的机器中间，显得又厚又笨。

English

I remember saving for three years in high school before finally buying a Sony Walkman with a radio, a recorder and a cassette player all in one. It was quite an advanced machine at the time. Boys of that era all seemed to have a Walkman dream of some sort. Then the world changed very quickly. Cassettes gave way to CDs, CDs to MiniDiscs, and then came MP3 players. Now everyone streams music on their phones. These old things seem to hold little more than scattered stories from when we were young.

I have to say, Sony took the design and craftsmanship of the Walkman to extraordinary heights. Even now, when I take some of these machines out to handle them, the quality still moves me. Most of the tapes from my childhood have gone mouldy and are no longer much use for listening, but I still like collecting Walkmans: models I loved back then but couldn't afford or couldn't find. In truth, these machines aren't much use in my hands now either. Inside a cassette Walkman is a small but essential rubber belt. With time, it deteriorates and stretches; some even turn into a sticky mess. And then the machine no longer runs.

When I was still living in Beijing, I picked up quite a few over the years. Thanks to the internet, there is now a chance to buy almost anything that seemed so precious when we were children. Of course, how much you are willing to pay determines the condition you can get. I have a friend in Beijing who has collected almost every Sony Walkman series in almost every colour, all brand new and boxed: cassette players, CD players and MiniDisc players. He didn't buy a home in Beijing. He put the money for one into his collection room instead. That counts as a rather noble ambition too, I suppose.

When it comes down to it, a Walkman is just for listening to music. These days, whatever song you want is a tap away. Everything is within reach. Back then, to hear a particular song, you had to hold down the rewind button, let go when you thought you were close, play a line or two, then rewind again if you had missed it. That feeling of searching, little by little, is gone now. There was another familiar move with cassettes: put a pencil through one of the spool holes, hold it up and swing the cassette around it to wind the tape a little faster. We thought that was great fun.

I can't listen to the tapes any more, but I have kept buying CDs. There are a lot at home, and I have hung a few particular favourites on the wall. To me, streaming music is ultimately something rather insubstantial. A song can disappear from a service just like that. A CD is an actual disc, in my own home. Whenever I want to listen, I can take it out. It will always be there. I probably thought the same thing when I was buying cassettes.

Actually, my first Walkman wasn't the one I saved up for myself. In my first year of high school, my father scrimped and saved to buy me Sony's most basic playback-only model. It was thick and big. This was 1996, and it cost more than two hundred yuan, when an ordinary city worker earned only four or five hundred a month. Compared with the machines I collected later, it was bulky and rough. But I can still clearly remember how excited I was that afternoon when I got it, as though something was about to begin that very day.

So many stories in my life, if I trace them back far enough, have something to do with music. First I listened to songs, then I played in a band and met a lot of people. Through those people came the career that followed. Perhaps it all began with that Walkman that cost a little over two hundred yuan. It is there in the photograph, at the far right of the second row from the bottom, looking thick and clumsy among all those finely made machines.
