A black-and-white camera

黑白相机

中文

这台相机，对我而言有一些不一样的情感。

其实，想买一台黑白的徕卡已经很久了。但是大家也知道，黑白的徕卡和彩色的徕卡价格差不多，黑白的却只能拍黑白。作为一个还比较讲究性价比的人，我之前一直觉得，这么做似乎少了些什么。

这个念头其实也挺矛盾的。我可以买很多相机，也很舍得花钱买这些大人的玩具，可一旦面对一台主动少了一种能力的相机，又会忍不住算起账来。同样的钱，为什么不能把彩色也买下来？哪怕平时不用，留着这个选择总是好的。大概买东西的时候，人很容易这么想：可以不需要，但最好还是有。

真正的契机，发生在2023年父亲去世前后。那段时间，我的心情一直特别灰暗。最终，办完父亲的后事，我买下了这台徕卡 M11 Monochrom。

到了那个时候，之前一直在算的那笔账，好像也没那么重要了。一台只能拍黑白的相机，究竟算是少了些什么，还是刚好只留下了我当时能面对的东西，我也说不清楚。只是，想了很久没有买的东西，最后是在那段时间买下来的。后来再拿起它，也就很难把这些事情完全分开。

不得不说，至少对我而言，黑白徕卡拍出来的照片，比把其他相机调成黑白模式拍出来的，要纯粹很多。几乎每一张，都会让我感觉到一些难以言说的感动。

这种感动很难拿参数解释，也未必能在屏幕上指给别人看。别人看到的可能就是一张普通的黑白照片，我看到的却还包括拍它时的自己。所以我说它纯粹，也不完全是在说画质。里面多少有一点我自己的偏心，这个我承认。

而且，因为它只能拍黑白，你没有选择。不是拿着一台彩色相机，今天选择黑白，明天还可以换回来。你别无选择，只能认真去寻找适合黑白的题材，然后很认真、很慎重地，按下那一下快门。

平时买设备，总希望它能做更多事情，最好什么情况都应付得来。但这台相机有意思的地方，恰恰是它有些事情就是做不了。你不用再替自己保留一个彩色的版本，也不用拍完之后比较哪一种更好看。这个决定，在你出门之前就已经做完了。剩下的，是眼前这个东西，你到底要不要拍。

这样一来，每一张照片似乎都有故事可言说。即使只是随手拍下街边的一个角落，或者房间里的一个物件，也好像有故事在向你倾诉。只是它们以你听不见的频率，在发出声音。

黑白有时候会让很普通的东西变得陌生一点。明明是一个没什么特别的角落，少了颜色之后，你反而愿意多看一会儿。说它真的有什么故事，可能也未必。也可能是看照片的人，把自己的故事放进去了。但对我来说，照片能让人停下来这么一会儿，就已经挺好了。

但我其实很少使用这台相机。

每次拿起来，感受到机身沉甸甸的重量，也会感觉到，黑白带给我的那种沉甸甸的情绪。当每天过得没心没肺，也比较快活的时候，其实很难想起来，要拿这台相机去捕捉一些很浓郁的情感。

所以，说它是我喜欢的一台相机，和说我经常用它，其实是两回事。喜欢一个东西，并不一定就会天天把它带在身边。有些东西，你知道它在那里就可以了。真要拿起来，又需要一点合适的心情。这么说似乎有点矫情，但这台相机对我而言，确实不只是出门时随手选的一件器材。

当然，让我不太愿意带它出门的，也有一个非常实际的原因：重。

买回来的时候，我给它搭配的是一支徕卡50毫米的夜神镜头，0.95的光圈。成像确实精彩绝伦，但缺点也非常明显。太重了。镜头可能比机身还重，这一套带出去，会成为一个非常明显的负担。

这也是玩器材很实际的一面。买的时候，会想象自己用它拍出什么样的照片；真要出门的时候，先想到的却可能是，今天要背着它走多久。镜头好不好，是一回事。你愿不愿意把它带出去，是另一回事。再好的镜头，如果因为太重总待在家里，那些想象中的照片也就一直只是想象。

为了让这台相机的利用率高一点，我后来把夜神卖掉，换了一支很简单的福伦达50毫米镜头。再后来，又买了一支徕卡原厂的75毫米 AA 镜头。

这支75确确实实不错。我记得价格大概只有那支夜神的三分之一，但在我自己的使用里，除了焦距不同，其他方面并没有觉得与50夜神有多大的区别。

当然，这就是我自己的感受，不是什么严谨的镜头比较。真要把照片放大了逐项去看，肯定还能说出很多不同。但我给自己换镜头，主要还是想让这台相机更容易被拿起来。夜神很好，这一点不用怀疑；只是对那时候的我来说，稍微轻一点，也是一种很重要的好。

说到这支夜神，卖掉它的时候，还挺有意思的。

以我自己买卖二手器材的经验，在中国买回来的相机和镜头，拿到新加坡用，有时还挺保值，甚至转手时能卖出更高的价格。这支夜神，就是其中一个例子。

具体挂了多少钱，我已经忘了。我记得还留了一点可以谈价的空间，结果买家完全没有还价，直接就定下了，问什么时候能过来拿。

我大概能理解。在新加坡买卖二手镜头，有些镜头确实一镜难求，夜神就不太容易碰到。真看到了，要先赶紧拿到手，价格反而成了其次的问题。

过来拿镜头的是一个小男生，看着可能还没参加工作，还在读书。他的母亲陪着他一起来。也许因为这是一笔不小的交易，所以妈妈要陪着。

男生从我手上接过镜头，装到自己的徕卡上试了试，然后跟妈妈说，OK。妈妈就拿出现金，数给我。

回头想想，我记住的就是试镜头、说OK、数现金这么几下。没有预先想象的那种讨价还价，这笔交易就这样完成了。挂在二手平台上的时候，它是一支镜头、一个价格；到了那一刻，它要跟着眼前这个人走了。

这件事情让我挺感慨的。在家庭经济条件允许的情况下，家人可以这样支持一个孩子的兴趣，给他好的，甚至最好的。

我当然不知道他们家平时是什么样，也不知道这个男生拍得怎么样。但看到妈妈陪着来，等他试好，再把钱付给我，还是会觉得，这个兴趣是被认真对待的。喜欢摄影这件事，本来就很难马上拿一个结果出来。有人愿意支持你先去试，先去拍，我觉得已经很难得了。

说实话，这支50夜神，我有时都觉得自己不配用。但这个孩子，在妈妈的支持下，把它买回去了。

说不配，多少也有一点开玩笑的意思。倒不是觉得这么贵的镜头一定要拍出多了不起的作品，才算交代得过去。只是我买过、用过，最后还是因为它太重而把它卖了。换一个人，也许他愿意一直背着，也许那些让我犹豫的重量，对他根本不是什么问题。想到这里，我还挺愿意把它交到他手上的。

他还有很长的人生可以慢慢拍。相信在未来那些漫长的日子里，他应该能用这支镜头，拍出很出色的作品吧。

English

This camera carries a different kind of feeling for me.

I had wanted a black-and-white Leica for a long time. But, as everyone knows, a monochrome Leica costs about as much as a colour one, while only being able to shoot in black and white. As someone who still cares about value for money, I always felt I would somehow be getting less.

There is a contradiction in that, of course. I can buy a lot of cameras, and I am quite willing to spend money on these grown-up toys. But put a camera in front of me that deliberately leaves out one capability, and I start doing the sums. For the same money, why not have colour too? Even if I do not use it, surely it is better to keep the option. It is an easy way to think when buying things: I may not need it, but I would rather have it.

What finally brought me to it was my father’s death in 2023. It was a very dark time for me. After his funeral and the arrangements that followed, I bought this Leica M11 Monochrom.

By then, the calculation I had been making no longer seemed so important. Did a camera that only saw in black and white leave something out, or did it simply leave me with what I could face at the time? I cannot quite say. I only know that this thing I had wanted for so long finally came into my life during those days. Picking it up afterwards, it became difficult to separate the camera from everything around its arrival.

I have to say that, to my eyes at least, the photographs from a monochrome Leica feel much purer than those taken with another camera set to black-and-white mode. Almost every frame gives me a feeling I find difficult to put into words.

That feeling is hard to explain through specifications, and I might not be able to point it out to someone looking at the screen. They might see an ordinary black-and-white photograph. I also see something of myself when I took it. So when I call it pure, I am not speaking entirely about image quality. There is some personal bias in that. I admit it.

And because it can only shoot in black and white, you have no choice. You are not carrying a colour camera that lets you choose black and white today and switch back tomorrow. There is no alternative. You have to look carefully for subjects that suit black and white, and then press the shutter with real care and deliberation.

Usually, when buying equipment, I want it to do more, preferably to cope with every situation. What interests me about this camera is precisely that there are things it cannot do. I do not have to keep a colour version in reserve, or compare the two afterwards to decide which looks better. That decision has already been made before I leave home. What remains is the thing in front of me, and whether I want to photograph it.

Somehow, every photograph seems to have a story to tell. Even a casual picture of a street corner, or an object in a room, feels as though something is speaking to you. Only at a frequency you cannot hear.

Black and white can make ordinary things feel a little unfamiliar. It may be an unremarkable corner, but without colour I find myself willing to look at it a little longer. Whether it really has a story of its own, I do not know. Perhaps the person looking at the photograph has put their own story into it. But if a photograph can make someone pause like that, I think it has already done quite enough.

But I actually use this camera very little.

Whenever I pick it up, I feel the weight of its body, and also the weight of the emotions that black and white brings me. When I am getting through my days quite happily, without thinking too much, it is hard to remember to reach for this camera and go looking for some particularly intense feeling.

Liking this camera and using it often are two different things. Being fond of an object does not necessarily mean carrying it with me every day. With some things, it is enough to know they are there. Actually picking them up takes a certain mood. That may sound a little sentimental, but this camera really is more than a piece of equipment I happen to choose on my way out.

Of course, there was also a very practical reason I did not take it out much: the weight.

When I bought it, I paired it with a Leica 50mm Noctilux, with that f/0.95 aperture. The images were wonderful, but its weakness was obvious. It was so heavy. The lens might even have weighed more than the body. Taking the whole thing out became quite a burden.

This is a very practical side of playing with equipment. When buying a lens, I imagine the photographs I could make with it. When it is time to go out, the first thought may instead be how long I will have to carry it. How good a lens is and whether I am willing to take it with me are separate questions. However good it may be, if its weight keeps it at home, those imagined photographs remain imaginary.

To give the camera a better chance of actually being used, I sold the Noctilux and replaced it with a simple Voigtländer 50mm. Later, I also bought a Leica 75mm AA lens.

That 75 really is very good. As I remember it, it cost roughly a third of the Noctilux. But in my own use, apart from the different focal length, I did not feel there was a great deal of difference in other respects.

Of course, that is only my own impression, not a rigorous lens comparison. If I enlarged the photographs and examined them point by point, there would surely be plenty of differences to discuss. But my main reason for changing lenses was to make this camera easier to pick up. The Noctilux was excellent; I had no doubt about that. At that point, though, being a little lighter was an important kind of excellence too.

Speaking of the Noctilux, selling it turned out to be quite interesting.

In my own experience buying and selling used equipment, cameras and lenses bought in China can sometimes hold their value quite well when you bring them to Singapore. You may even sell them for more than you paid. This Noctilux was one such example.

I have forgotten exactly what price I listed it at. I remember leaving a little room for negotiation, but the buyer did not bargain at all. He agreed straight away and asked when he could collect it.

I could understand that. Some lenses are hard to find on Singapore’s second-hand market, and a Noctilux does not come along very often. When one appears, you want to get it before someone else does. The price becomes a secondary concern.

The person who came to collect it was a young man. He looked as though he might still be a student, not yet working. His mother came with him. Perhaps she was there because it was quite a large purchase.

He took the lens from me, fitted it to his Leica, and tried it out. Then he said to his mother, “OK.” She took out the cash and counted it into my hand.

Looking back, what I remember are those few actions: trying the lens, saying OK, counting the cash. Without any of the bargaining I had expected, the sale was done. On the second-hand platform it had been a lens and a price. Now it was going home with this particular person.

That stayed with me. When a family can afford it, they can support a child’s interest in this way, giving him something good, perhaps even the best.

Of course, I knew nothing about their family life, or how good a photographer the young man was. But seeing his mother come with him, wait while he tested the lens, and then pay for it made me feel that his interest was being taken seriously. Photography is not something for which you can immediately produce a result to justify everything. Having someone willing to support you while you try, while you take photographs, seems precious enough to me.

Honestly, there were times when I felt I hardly deserved to use that 50mm Noctilux. But this young man, with his mother’s support, took it home.

When I say I hardly deserved it, I am partly joking. I do not think an expensive lens has to produce extraordinary work to justify its existence. It is just that I bought it, used it, and ultimately sold it because it was too heavy. Someone else might be happy to carry it all the time. The weight that made me hesitate might not matter to him at all. Thinking of it that way, I was quite happy to hand it over.

He has a long life ahead of him in which to take photographs. I imagine that, in all those years to come, he will make some very fine pictures with that lens.

我当然不知道他们家平时是什么样，也不知道这个男生拍得怎么样。但看到妈妈陪着来，等他试好，再把钱付给我，还是会觉得，这个兴趣是被认真对待的。喜欢摄影这件事，本来就很难马上拿一个结果出来。有人愿意支持你先去试，先去拍，我觉得已经很难得了。

说实话，这支50夜神，我有时都觉得自己不配用。但这个孩子，在妈妈的支持下，把它买回去了。

说不配，多少也有一点开玩笑的意思。倒不是觉得这么贵的镜头一定要拍出多了不起的作品，才算交代得过去。只是我买过、用过，最后还是因为它太重而把它卖了。换一个人，也许他愿意一直背着，也许那些让我犹豫的重量，对他根本不是什么问题。想到这里，我还挺愿意把它交到他手上的。

他还有很长的人生可以慢慢拍。相信在未来那些漫长的日子里，他应该能用这支镜头，拍出很出色的作品吧。
